Gamified task manager: how to pick one that keeps working

A gamified task manager attaches a reward to finishing work, so the list feels less like a ledger of things you have not done. The idea is old and mostly sound. The execution is where these apps separate, and most people pick on the art style rather than on the mechanic.

This guide covers what makes a reward hold up over months, how the main approaches differ, and what to check before you commit your task list to one.

What a reward mechanic has to get right

The reward has to track real work. If you can farm it by logging trivial tasks, the number stops meaning anything, and you will know that before the app does. The reward should come from completed tasks or finished focus sessions, and it should scale with effort.

The task layer has to be a real task layer. Gamification on top of a weak to-do list is a weak to-do list with a progress bar. You need due dates, repeats, reminders, priorities and a daily view you trust.

It has to survive a bad week. Everyone misses days. The question is what the system does about it, and that single design choice decides whether you are still using the app in March.

The four mechanics, and who each one suits

  • Points and levels (XP). Abstract and flexible. Works if numbers motivate you, and fades for people who need something to look at.
  • A growing thing. A tree, a pet, a city. The reward is visual and cumulative, which suits people whose effort otherwise feels invisible.
  • Social stakes. Parties, guilds, shared quests. The strongest pull for people who show up for others more reliably than for themselves, and pressure for people who don't.
  • Loss mechanics. Streaks that break, plants that die, characters that lose health. Effective at first, and the reason most people eventually quit.

The failure mode: punishment dressed as motivation

Most gamified apps eventually punish you for stopping. The streak resets, the pet gets sad, the avatar loses HP.

That works while you are winning. The problem is what it teaches when you are not. Miss two days in a genuinely bad week and the app's response is to take something away, exactly when the cost of reopening it is already highest. Then you don't reopen it.

A reward that only accumulates has the opposite property. A quiet week costs you nothing except a week of not growing, which is already true.

So before you commit: find out what the app does on the day you miss. That answer matters more than the feature list.

The main options

Names people compare, with what each is built for. Check current pricing and features on each app's own page, they change.

  • Habitica turns habits, dailies and to-dos into an RPG, with parties and guilds. The strongest social layer in the category. Character health drops when you miss tasks, so the stakes are real in both directions.
  • Forest plants a tree for each focus session and it dies if you leave the app. A clean, recognizable focus loop with no task management, so most people pair it with a separate list.
  • Finch wraps self-care routines around a pet you raise. Gentler in tone than the RPG apps, and aimed more at daily habits than at project work.

There are also newer entries adding AI-generated rewards, RPG classes or per-task visuals. Worth trying, but check how young the app is before you move your whole task list into it.

Dovilo: a city built from finished work

Dovilo pays completed work out into a city.

Finish a task or a focus session and you earn bricks. Spend bricks in the Build Market on houses, offices, parks, roads and decorations, then place them on an isometric grid. Bricks are the only currency, and the only way to earn them is to finish something.

Nothing decays, and no streak punishes you for a quiet week. The city you built in January is still there in December. It is a record of the last few months, not a score you can lose.

The task layer

Due dates, repeats, reminders, priorities, stars and flags. One view picker covers My Day, Important, Planned, Flagged, all tasks and your own lists, each with a live count. Tasks group by Today, Tomorrow and No date.

It is not a project management tool. It covers what one person needs to plan a day.

Focus and ambience

Three timer modes: a single session of any length, a Pomodoro cycle of 25 minutes on and 5 off with a longer break every 4 rounds, and a session tied to one task. A breathing exercise and a countdown ease you in, the timer runs in the background and with the phone locked, and Do Not Disturb can silence other apps for the length of the session.

The Default ambience scene is free and silent. The Focus Ambience Pack adds five full-screen scenes: Rainy City, Midnight, Dusk, Snow and Fog, each with its own weather, skyline tint and sound. The sound mixer blends up to 3 of 12 layers at once. It is ambience, not music. No melody, no beat.

For developers: the MCP server

Dovilo Desktop (Windows and macOS, part of Pro) runs a local MCP server that gives AI coding agents their own task list, on a monthly quota. Nine tools, three read and six write, read and update only. No create tool and no delete tool.

Your personal list is not on that tool surface at all, and an agent is scoped to one project and its brief. MCP traffic stays local over stdio, with no Dovilo endpoint in the agent's request path. The AI tasks sync to your phone through Pro cloud sync, so you can watch steps and token cost while the agent runs.

Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, Windsurf, Codex CLI and Gemini CLI have presets; any other MCP client connects with JSON. Signed webhooks push status changes into Slack, Linear, n8n or a custom endpoint. Details: dovilo.app/ai and the MCP setup guide.

How to choose

  • You show up for other people more than for yourself. Habitica's parties and guilds are the most developed social layer in the category.
  • You just need to stay off your phone. Forest does one thing, and does it in two taps.
  • You want a gentler, habit-shaped system. Finch is built around routines and tone rather than stakes.
  • You want the work to leave something behind. A city that only grows fits people whose effort otherwise disappears at the end of the week.
  • You also run coding agents. Keep their tasks on a separate surface from your own. Dovilo does that locally on Desktop, with no create or delete tools.

Frequently asked questions

What is a gamified task manager? A to-do app that attaches a reward to finishing work: points, a growing thing, a character, a city. The goal is to make starting easier and finishing feel like it counted.

Does gamification actually work? For some people, and it depends on the mechanic. Rewards tied to completed tasks and real focus time hold up better than abstract counters. Loss mechanics motivate early and cost you later, when a missed week turns into a deleted app.

What happens in Dovilo if I stop for two weeks? Nothing. The city stays exactly as you left it. There is no streak to lose and nothing decays.

Can I earn bricks without doing real work? Bricks come from completed tasks and completed focus sessions, and focus bricks are paid on duration, so the fastest way to earn is to actually work. Only finished Pomodoro rounds count toward the daily counter.

Is Dovilo free? Yes, on iOS and Android, with unlimited tasks, categories and focus sessions, and no ads. Pro adds sync, advanced statistics, the desktop app and both content packs, from $2.49/month with a 7-day trial started in the app.

Can I use it with Claude Code or Cursor? Yes, on Desktop with Pro. Nine tools, read and update only, on a task list separate from your personal one, with the agent scoped to a single project.

Which is better for habit tracking specifically? Habitica and Finch are built around daily habits. Dovilo is built around tasks and focus sessions, with repeats for the things you do regularly.

Plain to-do lists are good at capture and bad at making you want to open them. A reward layer fixes the second part, as long as the reward tracks real work and survives a bad week.

Pick the mechanic that matches what motivates you, and check what the app does on the day you miss. Dovilo is free on the App Store and Google Play.
