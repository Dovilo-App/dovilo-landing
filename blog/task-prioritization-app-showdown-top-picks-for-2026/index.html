Task prioritization apps: how to rank your day and pick a tool

Most people looking for a task prioritization app are not missing a feature. They have a list of thirty things, all of which look mildly important, and no method for deciding which one to open first.

An app can support that decision. It cannot make it for you. This guide covers three ranking methods that hold up in a real week, what an app has to do to support them, and how the main options differ.

Prioritization is a decision, not a feature

A priority field is a label. It becomes prioritization only when something downstream changes because of it: a view that sorts by it, a daily list that pulls from it, or a rule that says you don't start item two before item one.

Apps with five priority levels and no such rule end up with every task marked high. The labels stop carrying information within a month, and you are back to scanning the whole list.

So the question to ask about any app is not "can I set a priority?" It is "does this app change what I see because of that priority?"

Three ranking methods that survive a real day

One non-negotiable, then everything else. Before you open anything, name the single thing that has to be finished today. Not three. One. If the day falls apart and that one thing is done, the day worked. This is the method most people need, because it forces a choice instead of a ranking.

Urgent versus important. The Eisenhower split still works: urgent and important goes first, important and not urgent gets scheduled, urgent and not important gets minimized, neither gets dropped. The value is not the grid. It is noticing how much of your list is urgent to someone else and important to nobody.

Cost of delay over effort. For each candidate, ask what it costs if you do it next week instead of today, then weigh that against how long it takes. High cost and low effort goes first. Low cost and high effort is usually the thing you have been rewriting your plan around for a month.

Pick one. Running all three is a fourth way to avoid starting.

The trap: ranking as procrastination

Reordering a list feels like work. It produces a visible change, it lowers anxiety for a few minutes, and it costs nothing.

A useful test: if you have ranked the list twice today and started nothing, the problem is not your priorities. It is the start. In that case the tool you need is a timer, not a better sort order.

This is why a prioritization app that includes a focus mechanism tends to outperform one that only ranks. The gap between deciding and doing is where the day gets lost.

What an app has to give you

Capture in under five seconds. If adding a task interrupts you, you will stop adding tasks, and the list stops reflecting the work.

One view that answers "what now". Not seven filters. One place where today's short list lives, built from your ranking.

A way to act on the top item. A timer, a session, a do-not-disturb mode. Something that turns the decision into a block of protected time.

An honest record. What you finished, not how many days you opened the app. Streaks measure attendance. A record of finished work tells you whether your ranking was any good.

The options

The usual comparison set, with what each is actually for. Pricing and feature details change, so check each tool's own page.

  • Todoist and Things 3 are mature personal task managers with strong capture, projects and priority levels. Things 3 is Apple only. Todoist ships an official MCP server, so agents can work against your tasks.
  • TickTick pairs a task list with a built-in Pomodoro timer and a calendar view, which covers both the ranking and the doing in one app.
  • Notion is a workspace you build your system inside. Maximum flexibility, and the setup is a project of its own. It ships an official MCP server.
  • Linear is issue tracking for software teams, with native agent support. Excellent for a team, heavy for personal daily planning.
  • Habitica is a habit RPG with parties and guilds. Built for recurring behaviour and social accountability rather than for ranking a workday.

Where Dovilo fits

Dovilo is a task manager with focus sessions and a city you build from finished work. For prioritization specifically, here is the relevant surface.

The prioritization surface

Tasks carry a priority level, a star, a flag, a due date, repeat, reminders and notes, and belong to lists you define.

One view picker replaces filter pills: My Day, Important, Planned, Flagged, all tasks and each of your lists, every one with a live count. Lists group by Today, Tomorrow and No date, and the Planned view groups by day.

In practice that maps onto the methods above. My Day holds the non-negotiable and whatever else you committed to today. Important and Flagged separate what matters from what is merely loud. Planned shows where the rest actually lands, which is usually the moment you notice you scheduled eleven things for Thursday.

Acting on the top item

Once the list is ranked, start a focus session bound to the task at the top. Three modes: a single session of any length, a Pomodoro cycle of 25 minutes on and 5 off with a longer break every 4 rounds, or a session tied to one task that runs until it is done.

A breathing exercise and a countdown ease you in, the timer keeps running with the phone locked, and Do Not Disturb can silence other apps for the length of the session.

Finished tasks and completed sessions earn bricks, which you spend in the Build Market on buildings, roads and decorations placed on an isometric grid. Nothing decays, and no streak punishes you for a quiet week. It is a record of finished work, not a score you can lose.

For developers: the agent layer

Dovilo Desktop (Windows and macOS, part of Pro) runs a local MCP server that gives AI coding agents their own task list, on a monthly quota. Nine tools, three read and six write, read and update only. No create tool, no delete tool.

Your personal list is not on that tool surface at all, and each agent is scoped to one project and its brief. When a decision has consequences, the agent sets the task to awaiting-user, writes a note and stops.

MCP traffic stays local over stdio, with no Dovilo endpoint in the agent's request path. The AI tasks sync to your phone through Pro cloud sync, so you can follow steps and token cost while the agent runs. Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, Windsurf, Codex CLI and Gemini CLI have presets; any other MCP client connects with JSON. Details: dovilo.app/ai and the MCP setup guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a task prioritization app? An app that helps you capture tasks and decide which to do next, usually through priority levels, due dates and a daily view. The useful ones change what you see based on how you ranked things.

What is the simplest prioritization method? Name one thing that has to be finished today, and do it first. Most systems are elaborations of that.

Why do I keep marking everything high priority? Because nothing downstream depends on the label. Priority only works when a view, a daily list or a rule about starting order changes because of it.

Does Dovilo have Eisenhower matrix or Kanban views? No. It has priority levels, stars, flags and the My Day, Important, Planned and Flagged views. You can run the Eisenhower split on top of those, but there is no matrix board.

Is Dovilo good for teams? No. There are no shared workspaces or team features. For team work, Linear or Notion fit better.

Can AI coding agents use Dovilo? Yes, on Desktop with Pro. Nine tools, read and update only, on a task list separate from your personal one, with the agent scoped to a single project.

Does Dovilo support data export? Not yet. It is on the public roadmap. If exporting your history matters to you, factor that in before you commit.

What is the difference between Dovilo and Habitica? Both attach a reward to finishing work. Habitica uses RPG mechanics and a social layer, with character health that drops when you miss tasks. Dovilo uses a city built from bricks, with no streaks and nothing that decays.

The best prioritization app is the one that gets you from the list to the first task fastest. Pick a method, keep today's list short enough to be a decision, and then start the clock.
